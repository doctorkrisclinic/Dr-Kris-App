The question we hear most: "Do I need to take something?"

A condom broke, oral sex, a slip on holiday, or a partner who has just told you they have HIV. They all end with the same question. The answer doesn't depend on how worried you are. It depends on four things in the guidelines: how long ago it happened · what kind of exposure it was · your partner's HIV status · whether you already take PrEP. This article sums up the criteria from the Thai Department of Disease Control, the CDC and the WHO, then lets you check your own situation in the self-assessment.

How PEP and PrEP differ, and which one you need

PEP · after exposure

  • Three HIV medicines, once a day
  • Start as soon as possible, ideally within 24 hours, no later than 72
  • Take the full 28 days
  • An emergency measure, not routine prevention

PrEP · before exposure

  • Two medicines in one tablet
  • Daily, or event-driven (2-1-1) for some people
  • Used for as long as the risk continues
  • Blood tests before starting and every 3 months

Do I need PEP? Criteria from the CDC and WHO

The CDC (2025) and WHO (2024) guidelines recommend considering PEP when all three apply.

1. Within 72 hours. After 72 hours there is very little evidence that PEP works, so starting it isn't recommended.
2. An exposure that can transmit HIV. Blood, semen, vaginal or rectal fluid reaching a mucous membrane or broken skin: anal or vaginal sex without a condom or with a broken condom, shared needles, a needlestick, or sexual assault.
3. A partner with HIV who isn't undetectable, or whose status is unknown. When the status is unknown, the doctor decides case by case, taking into account whether the partner belongs to a group with higher HIV prevalence.

Recommended PEP is a three-drug regimen built around dolutegravir or bictegravir, taken once a day for 28 days. You have an HIV test before starting (you can start without waiting for the result), then repeat tests at 4–6 weeks and 12 weeks. Read more about PEP

Who should take PrEP? Thai 2021 and CDC criteria

Thailand's 2021 PrEP service guideline recommends PrEP for people who can't avoid HIV risk for now. The CDC (2021) looks at the past 6 months. Any one of these counts:

  • Anal or vaginal sex without a condom with a partner of unknown status, or with a partner who has HIV and isn't yet undetectable
  • Syphilis, gonorrhoea or chlamydia in the past 6 months
  • Having used PEP (guidelines advise discussing PrEP with everyone who comes for PEP, especially repeat users)
  • Sharing needles or injecting equipment
  • Men who have sex with men, trans women, sex workers, or people using drugs during sex (chemsex) who don't use condoms consistently

Daily PrEP lowers the chance of getting HIV from sex by about 99% when taken consistently. At the start it takes about 7 days to reach full protection for anal sex, and about 21 days for vaginal sex or injecting. Event-driven 2-1-1 dosing is only for men and people assigned male at birth who don't take estradiol and don't have hepatitis B. Read more about PrEP

When you don't need medicine

Usually no PEP needed

  • Partner with HIV who has been undetectable on treatment for ≥6 months (U=U), for sexual exposure
  • You already take PrEP consistently
  • Kissing, skin contact, mutual masturbation, or sex with a condom that stayed intact
  • Oral sex with no sores or bleeding (very low risk)

Missed the PEP window?

  • Test for HIV now and again after the window period
  • Test for syphilis, gonorrhoea and chlamydia
  • If you may be exposed again, start PrEP after a negative HIV test
  • Fever, rash or sore throat within 2–4 weeks: see a doctor

    Frequently asked questions

    The condom broke. Do I need PEP?

    If your partner's status is unknown, or they have HIV and aren't undetectable, and it's within 72 hours, PEP should be considered. Contact a doctor quickly.

    Do I need PEP after oral sex?

    The risk is very low and PEP usually isn't needed. With sores in the mouth, bleeding or ejaculation in the mouth, the doctor may assess it case by case.

    It's been more than 72 hours. Can I still take PEP?

    Starting PEP is no longer recommended. Get tested for HIV and other STIs, repeat the tests as advised, and consider PrEP if you're still at risk.

    I'm on PrEP but missed doses. Do I need PEP?

    It depends on how many doses you missed and when. If you take it consistently, PEP usually isn't needed. If you missed several days, send your details to the doctor within 72 hours.

    My partner has HIV but is undetectable (U=U). Do I need medicine?

    No PEP is needed for sexual exposure. If you have other partners or aren't sure about your partner's results, talk to the doctor about PrEP.
