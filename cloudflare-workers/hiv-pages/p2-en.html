HIV Risk Check: How Risky Was It, and What Are the Odds per Act?

Dr. Krida Uakridathikarn (Dr. Kris) · Medical licence 48387
Family physician, trained in clinical sexology and sexual medicine · Reviewed 19 November 2026 · 6-min read
In short
  • The chance of HIV per act varies hugely by type of exposure. Receptive anal sex carries the highest risk, oral sex very little.
  • Condoms, PrEP and a partner who is undetectable on treatment (U=U) cut the risk sharply or to zero.
  • The 4-colour check below gives you a level of Low · Some · High · Urgent that you can send to the doctor on LINE.

HIV risk per act when the partner has HIV and isn't on treatment

These figures come from the systematic review the CDC uses (Patel et al., 2014) and assume a partner with HIV who is not on treatment. If the partner is undetectable on treatment, the risk through sex is zero.

Type of exposurePer 10,000 acts
Receptive anal sex138 (about 1.4%)
Sharing needles to inject drugs63
Needlestick23
Insertive anal sex11
Receptive vaginal sex8
Insertive vaginal sex4
Oral sex, kissingVery low

The numbers per act look small, but risk adds up with repeated exposure and rises further with the factors below.

What raises the risk

  • A partner in early (acute) infection, when the amount of virus in the blood is very high
  • Sexually transmitted infections such as syphilis, gonorrhoea or herpes sores, in you or your partner
  • Bleeding, sores or tearing during sex, including sexual assault
  • Using drugs during sex (chemsex), which leads to less protection and more partners

What lowers the risk

Cuts the risk sharply or to zero

  • U=U: a partner undetectable on treatment for ≥6 months doesn't pass HIV on through sex
  • PrEP taken consistently: about 99% lower risk
  • Condoms used every time, correctly

After a slip

  • PEP within 72 hours, for 28 days
  • HIV and STI tests as scheduled
  • If risks keep happening, move to PrEP

What the 4 colours in the check mean

Low A very low chance of HIV. Medicine usually isn't needed.
Some There is some risk. Get tested and consider prevention.
High You meet the criteria for PrEP, or need close follow-up testing.
Urgent A risk within 72 hours. PEP needs to be assessed now.

The risk level tells you how risky the exposure was. Whether you need medicine also depends on timing and your partner's status. See the decision criteria in Do I need PEP or PrEP?

    Frequently asked questions

    Can you get HIV from oral sex?

    It's possible but the chance is so low most studies can't measure it. The risk goes up with sores in the mouth, bleeding or ejaculation in the mouth.

    A condom broke once. What's the percentage risk?

    It depends on the type of exposure and your partner's status. For example, receptive anal sex with a partner who has untreated HIV is about 1.4% per act. If it's within 72 hours, get assessed for PEP.

    Is sex with someone who has HIV but is undetectable risky?

    HIV isn't passed on through sex (U=U) when the partner stays on treatment and has been undetectable for at least 6 months.

    My result says "Urgent". What should I do?

    Send your result to the doctor on LINE straight away, or go to the nearest hospital emergency department. The sooner PEP starts, the better it works.
