Gale (Cyproterone 50 mg) Dosage: How Many Tablets a Day? Blood Targets and Monthly Cost

The dose you take every day matters more than the price of the box. Taking more than you need adds risk and cost. This guide compares common doses with current guidelines and works out the monthly cost for each.

Dr. Krisada Uakridathikarn (Dr. Kris)Board-certified Family Medicine · Medical licence 48387 · Certificate in Clinical Sexology and Sexual Medicine · Reviewed 4 Nov 2026 · 7 min read
A common question

A pharmacy told me to take one Gale tablet twice a day. Others take one a day. Which is right, and is that too much?

Most pharmacies go by the label, but gender-affirming therapy uses different doses. This guide compares doses with current guidelines and works out the monthly cost.

Key points

  • Gale is cyproterone acetate 50 mg, Thai FDA reg. no. 1A 18/68(NG), the same active ingredient as Androcur. Compare: Androcur vs Gale.
  • Two tablets a day (100 mg) is more than needed for gender-affirming therapy. That is the label dose for other indications. One tablet a day (50 mg) is the top of the older guideline range.
  • Newer evidence shows about 10–12.5 mg a day (about ¼ tablet) suppresses testosterone as well as higher doses when combined with estradiol.
  • At a low dose one box lasts about 40 days, so the monthly cost is several times lower than taking cheap tablets at a high dose.
  • Blood tests every 3 months in the first year. Targets: testosterone < 50 ng/dL, estradiol 100–200 pg/mL.

“Doctor, the pharmacy told me to take Gale twice a day and I have been doing it for over a year. Is that OK?” I hear this a lot. Gale is the right medicine, but the dose is often higher than needed, which adds risk and cost without a better result.

How many Gale tablets a day? Is 1 or 2 tablets too much?

The cyproterone 50 mg label says one tablet twice a day because its registered indications are other conditions, such as prostate cancer or severe hypersexuality. It was not written for gender-affirming therapy.

Daily doseEqualsCurrent evidence view
100 mg1 tablet twice a dayMore than needed for gender-affirming therapy; high cumulative risk
50 mg1 tablet once a dayTop of the Endocrine Society 2017 range (25–50 mg); now considered higher than needed
25 mg½ tabletBottom of the older range
10–12.5 mgabout ¼ tabletThe ENIGI study found similar testosterone suppression to higher doses when combined with estradiol

In 2020 the European Medicines Agency (EMA) advised using the lowest effective dose of cyproterone, because meningioma risk rises with cumulative dose. WPATH SOC-8 follows the same principle.

Doses in this article come from research and guidelines, for understanding only. This is not advice to split tablets or reduce your dose yourself. Dose changes should be made by your doctor with blood tests to confirm testosterone stays suppressed.

Do you need estrogen with Gale?

Gale suppresses male hormones, but the feminizing changes people want, such as breasts, skin and body shape, come mainly from estradiol. The standard regimen depends on whether you still have testes.

SituationRegimen doctors usually recommend
No surgery yet, testes presentEstradiol + an antiandrogen at the lowest effective dose
After orchiectomy or gender-affirming surgeryEstradiol alone; no antiandrogen needed
  • Oral estradiol (e.g. Estrofem): easy to use; guideline doses are about 2–6 mg a day, adjusted by your doctor to blood results.
  • Estradiol gel: bypasses first-pass liver metabolism and carries lower clot risk; suits older people, smokers or those with vascular risk.
  • Injectable estradiol: given by a healthcare professional; dose and interval set by your doctor from blood results.
  • Birth control pills containing ethinylestradiol are not recommended for gender-affirming therapy because their clot risk is higher than estradiol.

Is Gale (cyproterone) safe? Side effects and who should be careful

RiskWhat to know
Blood clots, strokeMainly from estrogen; higher in smokers, older people, overweight, inactive, or those with past clots. Your doctor may choose gel over tablets.
MeningiomaLinked to cyproterone dose and duration; do not use if you have had one.
LiverLiver enzymes may rise; test as scheduled, extra care with liver disease or heavy drinking.
High prolactinMay cause nipple discharge or headache; found on blood tests.
Lipids, glucose, blood pressureHormones can affect these; diabetes, hypertension or high lipids must be well controlled alongside.
MoodCyproterone can cause low mood or fatigue; tell your doctor if it affects daily life.
Thick blood (CBC)Mainly seen with testosterone therapy (trans men). The clinic checks blood count before every hormone regimen.

Go to hospital or call 1669 immediately for one-sided leg swelling or pain, sudden chest pain or breathlessness, a severe unusual headache, seizure, vision changes, limb weakness, or yellow skin or eyes.

Frequently asked questions

What is Gale? Is it a birth control pill?

Gale is cyproterone acetate 50 mg, an antiandrogen that lowers male hormones. It is not a contraceptive. In trans women it is used with estradiol to suppress testosterone, under a doctor’s supervision.

Is 2 tablets of Gale a day too much?

For gender-affirming therapy it is usually more than needed. 100 mg a day is the label dose for other indications. Newer evidence shows about 10–12.5 mg a day suppresses testosterone similarly when combined with estradiol.

I have taken 1 Gale tablet a day for a long time. What should I do?

Do not stop or reduce it yourself. See a doctor for testosterone, estradiol, liver and prolactin tests, then reduce gradually with blood test follow-up.

What are the hormone blood targets for trans women?

Per the Endocrine Society guideline, testosterone below 50 ng/dL and estradiol around 100–200 pg/mL, checked every 3 months in the first year, then 1–2 times a year.

Why does Gale cost more at the clinic than at a pharmacy?

The clinic price includes physician care: dose assessment, medication review, blood test interpretation and ongoing follow-up. At a low dose one box lasts about 40 days, so the monthly cost can be lower than buying cheap tablets and taking a high dose.

Where can I get Gale in Thailand?

Gale is dispensed under medical or pharmacist supervision. At Dr. Kris Clinic in Surat Thani we do not sell it online; it is prescribed only after a doctor's assessment, in person or by telemedicine nationwide. Start by signing up as a member and contacting LINE @dr.krisclinic. Existing patients can request a refill. See the Gale page.

Should Gale be used with estradiol?

Yes, for people who still have testes the standard regimen is estradiol plus an antiandrogen. After orchiectomy, estradiol alone is used.
